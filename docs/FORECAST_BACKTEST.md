## Method

- **Data:** the real KSP record only (seeded rows excluded); it ends 2024-03-10.
- **Window:** forecasts whose target weeks fall between 2023-10-02 and the week of 2024-03-04 — 23 weekly origins.
- **Rolling origin:** refitted every 4 weeks (6 refits), each on data strictly before its origin; between refits each week is forecast from the weeks up to it. Nothing is tuned on the scored window.
- **Capture:** share of all crime in the horizon that fell in the top 5% of units (units with any FIR in the prior 52 weeks), ranked by the forecast — compared with ranking by the last 4 weeks, by the last 52 weeks, and by what actually happened (the ceiling).
- **PAI** (Predictive Accuracy Index, Chainey 2008) = % of crime caught ÷ % of land flagged, at 5% of land (209,325 km² in the land mask). Beat areas are Voronoi estimates from their median centroids — the data carries no boundaries — so PAI is approximate. In brackets: the same for last-4-weeks hotspots.
- **Bands:** share of outcomes inside the 80% and 90% ranges.
- **Deviance gain:** Poisson deviance vs the baseline (seasonal-naive + EWMA); MAE shown as model / baseline. Calibration = Σ forecast ÷ Σ actual.

## Reading this honestly

- Forecasts count **reported** FIRs. An area that under-reports looks safe.
- Patrolling where the map points records more FIRs there, which the next forecast reads as more crime. Rotate some patrols into low-forecast beats.
- The window is one winter (Oct–Mar). Seasonal behaviour in other months is learned from 2019–2023 but not scored here.
- Categories with too few FIRs fall back to the baseline; their rows above measure the baseline.
